Letter of Introduction

A Shared Calling

Our journey into teaching began with our own time as students. Keren and I both struggled at school. We often felt unheard and a little lost, and we kept asking whether there was another way, a better way, to teach. I remember wishing for a teacher who could help me see things differently. In many ways, the work we do today grows out of that question.

For us, education is a way of serving God. We believe every student is made in God's image and deserves to be seen, heard and helped to flourish. A Christian school can be a place where learning, character and faith grow together, and that is the kind of community we want to give ourselves to.

Along the way we have been blessed by wonderful people: teachers, colleagues, mentors and Christian educators who shaped our thinking and passed a little of themselves on to us. We try to carry that forward wherever we go. At Hebron School in India and now at Weihai Zhongshi Foreign School in China, we have tried to do our best with what is in front of us: to teach well, to support the colleagues around us, and to help each school grow. For three years before coming to China, we also spent our holidays training teachers in schools with very few resources, and we learned as much from them as they did from us.

Keren has a special heart for the students who find school hardest: children with learning differences and those learning in a new language. Her patience helps them find their confidence, and her classroom is a place where every learner belongs.

Becoming parents to Trevor and Raquel has widened our understanding of what a school can be. Watching them grow, we have seen how much it matters that children are known, cared for and given room to thrive. We long for them, and for every student we teach, to grow up in a community like that.

We are still learning. We have gathered some valuable lessons along the way, but the journey continues, and we would love to keep growing and contributing as part of your school community. Thank you for taking the time to get to know us. We would be grateful for the opportunity to talk further.

Warm regards,
Jesus Glen Joshua

Curriculum

Bringing Computing to a new school

WZFS had never taught Computing, so I've been building it up from Grade 2 to Grade 10, one step at a time. Many of my students are learning in English, so I give them key words, sentence frames and model answers to help them show what they know. I also helped bring a Bible curriculum into PSHE.

Moving schools online

Helping teachers through change

At Hebron, COVID meant moving the whole school online almost overnight. I set up Google Workspace and Moodle and helped colleagues one by one. At WZFS I led the move to Microsoft 365. I've learned that people need support more than they need new tools.

Educational Technology

Making everyday work simpler

When I arrived at WZFS, students had no IDs and report cards were typed by hand. After six months of paperwork, the school now has Microsoft 365 for Education, every student has an ID, and I built a reporting app where teachers enter marks, comments and awards, and print report cards and certificates. I'm now working with the admin team to shape student transcripts from the same system, and with a committee on a goal-setting system students and parents can follow.

Educational Philosophy

What We Believe About Education

Empowering Teachers (The ZPD)

Our journey began by challenging traditional teaching methods, but it evolved into a deeper understanding: many teachers genuinely want to be good but struggle with how to adapt. We focus on scaffolding the educational environment and making learning visible, ensuring every student is challenged right at their Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD).

Volunteering & Multiplication

We regularly spend our holidays volunteering in schools across India, training teachers who operate with limited resources. By equipping educators to adopt practical, inclusive teaching methodologies and digital tools, we multiply our impact far beyond our own individual classrooms.

Technology as a Tool

Technology has changed how students learn, but it cannot form their character. Honesty, patience, responsibility and care are shaped by people. We see the teacher as a character-driven facilitator who scaffolds the learning: helping students narrow down what matters, bridging the gap between what they know and what they can do, and then letting technology empower their learning. In this way students grow as thinkers and creators, not just consumers. That careful shaping is the teacher's part, and only a teacher can do it.
